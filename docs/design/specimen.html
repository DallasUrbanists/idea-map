Let text wrap naturally

The specimen inherits text-wrap: pretty from the body for paragraphs, headings, lists, descriptions, captions, table prose, and other text that can wrap. You should allow multiline labels and controls to grow, rather than inserting manual line breaks or truncating essential words.

You should keep reading lines around 45–70 characters where space allows, using a limit such as 65ch; narrow cards can have shorter lines. Pixel equivalents assume a 16 px root font size, and rem-based sizes respect reader settings.

Short color-chip/code pairs stay together. Single-line fields keep native behavior, and literal preformatted code keeps its whitespace; browsers without support for pretty use ordinary wrapping.

06 / CORNER RADIUS

Shape follows the component's role

Rounded corners give related elements a consistent shape. You should choose from the shared radius scale and keep that choice stable across themes, screen sizes, and interaction states.

0 · square

Joined edges

border-radius: 0

Tables, map canvases, and reference color chips stay square; joined groups round only exposed outer corners.

8 px · small

Controls, labels, and compact surfaces

--radius-small

Buttons, fields, labels, selection highlights, theme pickers, swatches, popovers, and dialogs share this radius.

12 px · medium

Content containers

--radius-medium

Reading cards and substantial panels frame a coherent piece of content without rounding every row.

Pill · fully rounded

Short metadata

--radius-pill

Standalone tags and counts can use a pill; ordinary buttons and fields retain the small radius.

The pixel values assume a 16 px root font size. You should keep an inner radius no larger than its container's: a 12 px card and an 8 px control with at least 4 px of inset form a consistent pairing.

You should clip full-bleed images at the container's exposed corners, rather than clipping the entire container and hiding focus outlines or floating content. Corner shape alone does not communicate clickability, selection, or status.

07 / DROP SHADOWS

Elevation explains what floats

Ordinary content stays flat. You should use a shadow only when a surface floats over other content, with an opaque surface and a visible border in both themes.

These static surface samples compare the three levels; they do not open a menu or modal.

Flat · document flow

--specimen-shadow-flat

No shadow

Reading cards, buttons, form fields, and selected items rely on spacing, surface color, and borders. This card uses the 12 px content-container radius.

Floating · above content

--specimen-shadow-floating

0 4px 12px

Popovers, menus, and controls floating over a map use the small shadow and 8 px radius. Their borders remain visible when shadows disappear.

Overlay · modal surface

--specimen-shadow-overlay

0 12px 32px

Dialogs use the larger shadow and 8 px radius. A real modal also needs a backdrop, a label, focus management, and the correct stacking order.

Light-theme shadows use dark slate at 14% opacity for floating surfaces and 22% for overlays. Dark-theme shadows use black at 32% and 48%; borders remain essential because a dark shadow can disappear into the page.

You should keep shadows stable on hover, press, selection, and disablement. The 3px focus outline with a 3px offset remains independent of shadows; borders and semantic state must also work when shadows are unavailable.

Shadow recipes are local specimen variables. The shared stylesheet provides the radius scale; these examples do not change application components.

08 / BORDERS

Quiet structure, clear controls

You should begin with spacing and surface color, then add a subtle 1 px border only where it clarifies grouping. A single shared divider is usually enough between adjoining items.

Subtle · optional structure

--specimen-border-subtle

Card outlines, section dividers, and table rules use a quiet 1 px edge. They support grouping without carrying essential state or control meaning.

You should use 2 px borders only for explicit selected or invalid emphasis, alongside a label or mark; reserve that space in all states to prevent layout shifts. Keyboard focus remains a separate 3 px outline with a 3 px offset.

You should keep rules solid, avoid doubled borders, and use no border when grouping is already clear. Decorative rules can be subtle; essential control boundaries, selection indicators, and focus must remain identifiable when decorative colors or shadows disappear.

09 / MARGINS & PADDING

Space shows what belongs together

You should use margin outside a component, padding inside it, and layout gap between siblings. One container owns each gap; related items stay closer together than separate groups.

You should use 4 px for an icon or chip beside its label, 8 px between labels and fields or neighboring actions, and 16 px between related content. Card and panel padding is 24 px on desktop and 16 px on narrow screens; page gutters are 32 px and 16 px respectively.

You should separate groups by 24–32 px and major sections by 48 px. Buttons and fields use 8 px block padding and 12–16 px inline padding while retaining at least a 44 px target; content and action rows reflow when text grows.

Let backgrounds and media touch edges

Cover media, maps, background bands, row backgrounds, and dividers can reach container edges. Reading text, field labels, and button groups retain their content inset.
